# Exact stopping inequality on the repeated action

On a history playing e₁, the feedback average is 1/2. Write r=bₜ/t for the normalized boundary. The lower and upper quantities are L=1/2−r and U=1/2+r. With ε=1/4 the rule stops positively when L > 3U/4, equivalently r < 1/14.

For a worked substitution at r=1/16, L=7/16 and 3U/4=27/64. The stopping inequality holds by 1/64. At equality r=1/14, the published comparison is strict, so this test does not stop. The stopping test uses U from the trajectory. The separate true target remains 3M*/4=9/16, above the played margin 1/2.

The original arithmetic replay gives conservative finite parameters c=1, π=1/2, α=1/8, with T=2³² and an integer dimension. The theorem shows that such a trapping event has probability at least 1/4, and extends to every fixed c and π using a suitable T and dimension. The 3D projection illustrates which vertices matter.
