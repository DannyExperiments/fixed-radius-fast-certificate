# Q966 source and prior-art review

**Verdict, 27 November 2026.** High confidence that the full-dimensional example addresses the catalog’s fixed-radius, fixed-optimism, unchanged-boundary specialization. This targeted review found no previous counterexample to that finite stopping guarantee. Related failures of Thompson exploration supply a precedent for trapping, and the construction specializes that issue to the exact FAST stopping test. Priority remains unestablished.

## Exact rule and source scope

Aditya Gangrade, Aldo Pacchiano, Clayton Scott and Venkatesh Saligrama, *Feasible Action Search for Bandit Linear Programs via Thompson Sampling*, [ICML 2025, PMLR 267, 18228–18256](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/gangrade25a.html). The practical-noise question is [§3.4, final PDF p. 9](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/gangrade25a/gangrade25a.pdf#page=9). The main text mentions a small Gaussian perturbation related to the empirical intuition. [Appendix B.1.2, p. 24](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/gangrade25a/gangrade25a.pdf#page=24) specifically suggests a uniform sphere of radius √c for constant c and an optimism setting around 1/2. The paper also uses an empirical optimism estimate and a refined stopping rule in some experiments. The construction keeps the boundary of [Lemma 7, p. 6](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/gangrade25a/gangrade25a.pdf#page=6) in [Algorithm 1, p. 5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/gangrade25a/gangrade25a.pdf#page=5), replacing the perturbation law by a fixed-radius sphere and substituting its exact deterministic norm bound. The symbol c in this release denotes the radius itself.

The shipped proof covers every positive fixed radius and every fixed π in (0,1], separately for internal confidence δ or δ/5. The empirical boundary refinement, Gaussian case, dimension-growing radii and dimension-dependent optimism inputs are outside this construction. In the bad event the returned action is feasible with positive margin 1/2. Its failure is below the promised near-optimal margin 9/16.

## Closest comparisons

- The fixed-radius rule falls outside the source’s proved concentration–optimism guarantee. [Theorems 8–9 and Appendix A.2.1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/gangrade25a/gangrade25a.pdf#page=7) use distributions with coordinate-scale optimism, including standard Gaussian and sphere radius of order √d. A fixed-radius sphere has coordinate second moment c²/d. Appendix B’s optimism experiments at d=9 cannot supply a uniform reliability guarantee.
- [Abeille and Lazaric, AISTATS 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/abeille17a.html) use optimism to bound regret. [Hamidi and Bayati, arXiv:2006.06790, §§3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.06790#S3) show underexploration for LinTS under misspecification, including noiseless feedback and a fixed action set example. They analyze finite-horizon trapping and regret under different policies with Gaussian draws. Here the repeated-action trajectory has a dimension-independent normalized boundary that decreases to zero. That boundary calculation yields the actual premature FAST certification event.
- [Gangrade et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/gangrade24a.html) develop different tests of linear feasibility. [Gangrade and Saligrama, NeurIPS 2025](https://doi.org/10.52202/085713-4062) consider constrained Thompson sampling and regret/safety. [Güçlü, Bose and Birge, arXiv:2609.32149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32149) study regret and inference quality over spherical action sets. Spherical action sets differ from a spherical perturbation law on the present simplex. These results do not supply the fixed-radius trajectory and its FAST stopping certificate.

The exact-title and author-version search located the PMLR final source and the author OpenReview copy. The proof does not claim failure of the theoretical FAST procedure under verified optimism, or failure of all small-noise methods.
