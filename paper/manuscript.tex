\begin{abstract}
Feasible Action Search via Thompson Sampling (FAST) has a proven reliability guarantee under an optimism condition. We show that keeping the published stopping boundary and using a dimension-independent spherical perturbation of any fixed positive radius can fail. For any fixed boundary optimism parameter, a noiseless one-constraint simplex forces positive stopping with a true margin below the promised threshold with probability at least one quarter.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Question and exact rule}
Gangrade, Pacchiano, Scott and Saligrama~\cite{GPSS} propose FAST to find a near-optimal feasible margin or certify infeasibility. Their theoretical guarantee assumes a concentration--optimism condition. They ask about reliability for smaller noise with the optimism parameter retained, and suggest uniform perturbations on a sphere of fixed radius in Appendix~B.1.2. We study Algorithm~1 with the boundary of Lemma~7 and that fixed-radius distribution.

With a single constraint vector \(\theta\in\mathbb R^d\), the margin is \(M(a)=\theta^\top a\), and the optimal margin is \(M^\star=\max_{a\in A}M(a)\). In the noiseless case the scalar feedback is \(S_t=M(a_t)\). The published quantities reduce to
\[
 V_t=I+\sum_{s<t}a_sa_s^\top,\qquad
 \widehat\theta_t=V_t^{-1}\sum_{s<t}a_s S_s,\qquad
 \omega_t=1+\sqrt{\tfrac12\log(2/\alpha)+\tfrac14\log\det V_t}.
\]
At each round the algorithm maximizes the linear score of
\(\widehat\theta_t+\omega_t V_t^{-1/2}\zeta_t\) over \(A\), with the \(\zeta_t\) independent and uniform on the sphere of radius \(c\). Here \(\alpha\) is the confidence parameter used internally. The dual variable for the single constraint is constant. Thus \(T_t=\sum_{s\le t}S_s\). For the deterministic concentration bound \(B(\cdot)=c\) and supplied optimism parameter \(\pi\in(0,1]\), the exact boundary is
\[
 b_t=\frac{(1+5c)\omega_t}{\min(\pi,1/2)}
       \left[\sqrt{4t\log(11\max(\log t,1)/\alpha)}
                 +\sum_{s\le t}\sqrt{a_s^\top V_s^{-1}a_s}\right].
\]
Set \(L_t=(T_t-b_t)/t\), \(U_t=(T_t+b_t)/t\). Positive stopping requires \(U_t>0\) and \(L_t>(1-\varepsilon)U_t\). It returns the average action \(a_{\rm out}\) and certificate \(L_t\). Reliability in a positive-margin instance requires \(M(a_{\rm out})\ge M_{\rm out}\ge(1-\varepsilon)M^\star\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\).

\begin{theorem}
Fix \(c>0\) and \(\pi\in(0,1]\), independent of dimension. There is a noiseless one-constraint instance on a full-dimensional simplex in the Euclidean unit ball where the above rule with \(\varepsilon=1/4\) and \(\delta=1/8\) stops positively and returns an action of margin \(1/2\) even though \(M^\star=3/4\), with probability at least \(1/4\). This holds when the rule uses \(\alpha=\delta\) and also when it uses \(\alpha=\delta/5\).
\end{theorem}

\section{The boundary passes on a repeated action}
Let \(\theta=(1/2,3/4,0,\ldots,0)\) and let \(e_1,e_2\) be standard basis vectors. The simplex will have vertices
\[
                 0,\ e_1,\ e_2,\ \eta e_3,\ldots,\eta e_d
\]
for some \(0<\eta\le 1\) chosen below. The vertices are affinely independent and in the unit ball. The vector \(\theta\) has norm \(\sqrt{13}/4<1\); any reward vector can be set to zero. Taking the feedback noise zero obeys the source's conditional sub-Gaussian assumption. The optimal margin is \(3/4\) at \(e_2\). Repeating \(e_1\) yields margin \(1/2<9/16=(1-\varepsilon)M^\star\).

On the all-\(e_1\) history, \(V_t\) is diagonal with first entry \(t\), \(\det V_t=t\), and the sum of widths equals \(\sum_{s\le t}s^{-1/2}\le2\sqrt t\). Therefore \(b_t/t\to0\) on this history for fixed \(c,\pi,\alpha\), independently of \(d\) and \(\eta\). Choose an integer \(T\ge2\) on this deterministic history with \(b_T/T<1/14\). Then \(T_t/t=1/2\) throughout the history and \(U_t>0\). At \(T\) the stopping inequality becomes
\[
                \frac12-\frac{b_T}{T}>
                \frac34\left(\frac12+\frac{b_T}{T}\right),
\]
which is equivalent to \(b_T/T<1/14\). The algorithm stops positively at or before \(T\) whenever it plays only \(e_1\) until stopping or \(T\).

\section{Probability of that history}
Set \(W=1+\sqrt{\log(2/\alpha)/2+\log(1+T)/4}\). On a history consisting of \(e_1\) plays, \(\omega_t\le W\) through \(T\). At each \(t\ge2\) on that history the ridge score of \(e_1\) is \((t-1)/(2t)\ge1/4\), and the others' ridge scores are zero. If \(|\zeta_{t,1}|,|\zeta_{t,2}|\le1/(16W)\), the perturbed score of \(e_1\) is at least \(3/16\), and that of \(e_2\) is at most \(1/16\). This uses \(V_t^{-1/2}\) diagonal with first entry \(1/\sqrt t\) and remaining entries one. For each additional vertex \(\eta e_j\) the score is at most \(\eta c W\). Thus for \(\eta cW<1/16\) the action is uniquely \(e_1\).

Draw a virtual sequence of all directions even after actual stopping. By symmetry \(\mathbb E\zeta_{t,j}^2=c^2/d\). Markov's inequality and a union bound over two coordinates and \(T-1\) rounds give
\[
 \Pr\{\text{a required coordinate bound fails in rounds }2,\ldots,T\}
 \le \frac{512(T-1)c^2W^2}{d}.
\]
Choose \(d\ge3\) with \(d>8192Tc^2W^2\) to make the bound less than \(1/16\).

At initialization the ridge estimate is zero, and the common scale \(\omega_1\) has no effect on the maximizing vertex. The event \(\zeta_{1,1}>\max(0,\zeta_{1,2})\) has probability \(3/8\): opposite signs with positive first coordinate contribute \(1/4\); the first coordinate wins half of the joint positive quadrant, contributing \(1/8\). The sphere coordinate law is continuous. Choose \(0<\eta\le1\) small enough to have both \(\eta cW<1/16\) and \(\Pr(0<\zeta_{1,1}\le\eta c)<1/16\). It follows that with probability at least \(5/16\),
\(\zeta_{1,1}>\max(0,\zeta_{1,2},\eta c)\), which makes \(e_1\) the unique initial action. Intersect with the later coordinate bounds. The probability is at least \(5/16-1/16=1/4\), and induction gives only \(e_1\) plays until stopping or \(T\). The positive stopping then returns \(e_1\). No scalar certificate can satisfy \(1/2\ge M_{\rm out}\ge9/16\). This proves the theorem for each internal confidence parameter.

\section{Comparison with prior results}
The theoretical FAST guarantee in~\cite[Theorem~9]{GPSS} uses a perturbation with verified optimism. A sphere of radius proportional to \(\sqrt d\) and an isotropic Gaussian are analyzed at different scales in that work. The present construction concerns the fixed-radius spherical option in the practical discussion and the unchanged Algorithm~1 boundary. The simulations in Appendix~B also consider a refined experimental boundary. Earlier Thompson sampling analyses use optimism to control regret~\cite{AL}, while examples of failed exploration give regret lower bounds or trapping under different policies~\cite{HB}. Here the all-\(e_1\) history crosses the actual FAST stopping threshold in finite time. This gives a failure of the stated near-optimal margin guarantee even though the returned action has strictly positive margin.
